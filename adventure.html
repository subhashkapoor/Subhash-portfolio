Overview

A full travel product, built from zero.

As design manager I set the direction, split the product between a team of three designers, ran critiques and kept web and mobile in step, from the first search widget to booking management.

The challenge

Booking travel is full of small doubts.

Dates, rooms, guests, price, cancellation, payment: every step is a chance to drop off. The experience had to answer each doubt at the moment it appears.

A

Too many fields up front

Search widgets asked for everything at once, before people had even picked a city.

B

Results that are hard to compare

Price, rating, cancellation and location were scattered across each card.

C

Checkout that forces an account

Guests left when asked to sign up before paying.

D

Two products, two experiences

Hotels and vacation rentals needed one consistent journey.

Principles

Four rules every screen had to pass

We studied leading travel platforms and mapped a universal flow, then a Japan-specific one, before designing each screen.

Search in steps

Destination, then dates, then guests, each in its own focused step.

Price with context

Price heatmaps on the calendar and "only 2 rooms left" where it matters.

Same card everywhere

One hotel card on home, list, map and wishlist.

Guests are welcome

Logged-in and guest checkouts designed side by side.

01
Search

From "where to?" to results in three steps

The search widget sits on the home page and guides people one decision at a time. The calendar shows high-price, low-price and weekend days, so choosing dates is also choosing a budget.

Decision

One step at a time

Each step owns the screen on mobile, so the keyboard and calendar never fight for space.

Decision

Prices on the calendar

Showing price levels on dates helps flexible travellers save without extra searching.

Decision

Recent searches first

Most people come back to finish a search, so it is the first thing on home.

02
Results & details

Compare stays on a list or a map

Results can be browsed as a list, a grid or a map. The details page answers the questions that block a booking: photos, room types, facilities, location and rules.

Decision

List, grid or map

Some travellers decide by price, some by location, so the view is their choice.

Decision

Urgency, honestly

"Only 2 rooms left" and free-cancellation badges appear only when true.

Decision

Details that end doubt

Facilities, policies and nearby places are grouped and scannable.

03
Booking & checkout

A checkout that works with or without an account

Checkout asks for the minimum: who is checking in and how to pay. Coupons and rewards points are applied in place, and guests never hit a sign-up wall.

Decision

Guest checkout first

Guest and member flows were designed side by side so neither is a second-class path.

Decision

Savings in view

Coupons and points show their effect on the total immediately.

Decision

Saved payment methods

Cards and UPI accounts sit in one list, ready to tap.

What I learned

Split work by journey, not by screen. Each designer owned a stage of the journey end to end, which kept flows coherent and reviews focused.

Design the guest path first. If checkout works without an account, it works for everyone.

Put price where the decision happens. Price on the calendar did more for budget travellers than any filter.
