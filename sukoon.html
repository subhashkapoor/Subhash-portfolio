Sukoon Insurance · UAE · iOS & Android

Insurance that fits in one hand.

Designing the Sukoon app: one place for UAE customers to link their motor and health policies, file a claim from the roadside, and follow it until it's paid.

Mobile app · 0→1Motor + Health6 design sprintsShipped to App Store & Play
Overview

Policies, claims and help were spread across phone lines, branches and email.

Sukoon customers held motor and health policies, but the everyday jobs around them (checking cover, proving it at a clinic, reporting an accident, chasing a claim) happened outside any single product.

We designed a native app that brings those jobs into one place. It works for people who already hold a Sukoon policy and for people who are only shopping, and it treats the moments that matter most (an accident, a hospital visit) as the primary use cases rather than edge cases.

The challenge

Insurance is used at the worst possible moment.

Nobody opens an insurance app for fun. They open it beside a dented car, at a pharmacy counter, or when a claim has gone quiet. Every screen had to work for someone stressed, outdoors, on one bar of signal.

A

Linking a policy meant knowing a policy number

Most customers don't. They do know their Emirates ID, and they carry their health card.

B

Claims were forms, then phone calls

A motor claim needs a police report, Mulkiya, licence and invoice. Typing all of that by hand on a phone is where people give up.

C

Status lived inside the call centre

Once submitted, a claim disappeared. Customers called to ask "where is it?"

D

Two audiences, one front door

Existing customers want their policies; prospects want a quote. The app had to serve both without making either feel lost.

Design principles

Four rules every screen had to pass

Scan, don't type

If the data is printed on a card or report, the camera reads it and the customer confirms it. Manual entry stays as the fallback.

Help is one tap away

A floating call button sits on every screen that could end in a stuck customer, from linking to claims.

Always show where I am

Numbered steppers, review screens and plain status chips replace silence after "Submit".

Calm by design

Sukoon means "calm". Line-art illustration, one accent colour and generous space keep stressful tasks feeling manageable.

01
First launch

Two doors at the front: customer, or not yet

The welcome screen asks one question instead of forcing a sign-up. Prospects get products and a quote; customers sign in with email or UAE Pass, the national digital ID most residents already use.

Decision

Emergency links before login

Roadside assistance, medical services, support and branches sit on the welcome screen. In an accident, a password reset is the last thing anyone needs.

Decision

UAE Pass as an equal option

Shown as a full-width button rather than a small "other ways" link, because it removes passwords for most residents.

Decision

Biometrics asked once

A single, skippable prompt after first sign-in, with a clear "not enabled" state if the device refuses.

02
Home

A home screen that changes once you link a policy

Before linking, home is a guide: link a policy, explore products, get a quote. After linking, it becomes your policy, with the health card front and centre and your claims listed with their status.

03
Link a policy

Link with what's already in your wallet

Instead of a policy number, customers link with their Emirates ID or health card number. The ID can be scanned, the illustrated card shows exactly where the number is, and an error explains what to try next.

04
Hero flow · Motor claim

From roadside to submitted, without typing a report

The motor claim is the flow that justifies the app. We split it into six visible steps and let the camera do the data entry: scan the police report, Mulkiya or driving licence, then confirm what the app read.

1Claim details2Police report3Repairer4Documents5Contact6Review
Decision

OCR with a human check

The scan fills the fields, but the customer always confirms. A "No" sends them to edit, never to a dead end.

Decision

Scenario-driven design

We mapped five Step 1 scenarios (owner driving, other driver, Mulkiya scan, licence scan, manual entry) and designed each path with its own confirmation sheet.

Decision

Review before commit

A full summary with inline "Edit" links replaces the anxiety of a blind submit, and catches typos before they become phone calls.

05
After submit

Every claim has a status you can read

Claims are grouped into active and closed, filterable by family member and period, and each opens to a detail page that answers the questions people used to call about: how much, what status, last updated when.

06
Service layer

Find anything, reach someone

Search understands tasks ("download orange card", "IBAN details"), notifications link to the next action, and network coverage lets a customer find an in-network clinic by emirate before they walk in.

What I learned

Design the error before the happy path. In a regulated product most of the work sat in rejected scans, mismatched IDs and failed links. We kept a dedicated error-scenario page and reviewed it every sprint.

Sign-off walkthroughs beat review comments. Walking stakeholders through a clickable prototype end to end surfaced gaps that static reviews missed, and gave us a clean gap analysis before build.

A design system pays for itself in hypercare. Post-launch fixes to upload, notification and status components rolled out across every flow at once.
