Overview

One developer, many audiences, and a market that runs on trust.

Dar Al Arkan, through Thimar Al Arabiya, wanted to grow as a real estate provider in Saudi Arabia with its own online platform: web, mobile and a CRM, delivered in phases.

The mobile app had to serve two very different groups from one front door. End users want to find a home without visiting twenty of them. Owners, brokers, developers and agents want to list properties, see who is interested and act on those leads quickly, often from a car between viewings.

The challenge

Big decisions, low trust, and two languages.

A home is the largest purchase most families make. People told us they worried about fraudulent sellers, hidden fees and photos that don't match reality, and professionals told us their tools were scattered.

A

Search forms that ask everything at once

Regional portals open with ten filters before you've typed a city. Our review found that a simple start, with detail added step by step, felt far better.

B

Listings people couldn't trust

Buyers wanted verified photos and video, real amenities and nearby schools and hospitals before they'd book a viewing.

C

Leads spread across phone, WhatsApp and spreadsheets

Brokers and agents had no single place to see who called, who messaged and which listing they asked about.

D

English and Arabic, on any connection

Every screen had to flip cleanly from left-to-right to right-to-left, and stay light for mobile users on slow networks.

Research

Five personas, seven competitors, one clear pattern

We started with stakeholder questions about goals, users and what success looks like, then built personas for buyers, renters, owners, sellers and brokers, and reviewed seven property portals in the region.

What research told us

Four rules every screen had to pass

Start simple, refine later

One search bar and three tabs (For Sale, For Rent, International). Detailed filters live in a sheet you open when you need them.

Show the real thing

Every listing leads with photos, video and a 360 view, plus amenities, so people can shortlist from the sofa.

Contact in one tap

Call and WhatsApp sit on every property card. That's how people in the Kingdom already reach a seller.

Pros work on the go

Leads, reports, agents and listings fit in a phone-sized dashboard, built for a broker between viewings.

01
First launch

Browse first, register when it's worth it

Anyone can search and browse without an account. The walkthrough speaks to both audiences, and registration asks one question first: are you a broker, an owner or a developer?

Decision

No login wall

Search, map and property details work signed out. Sign-in only appears when you register interest or list a property.

Decision

Role before form

Picking broker, owner or developer first means each sign-up asks only for what that role needs.

Decision

Two promises, two cards

The walkthrough gives each audience its own card, so neither feels the app isn't for them.

02
Hero flow · Search

A search bar and three tabs, not ten dropdowns

Research showed that long filter forms put people off. Home opens with one search bar, For Sale / For Rent / International tabs and four property types. Detailed filters sit in a sheet, and every applied filter shows as a removable chip.

Decision

Tabs over dropdowns

Buy, rent or international is the first thing people decide, so it's always visible rather than hidden in a menu.

Decision

Chips show what's applied

A row of filter chips under the search bar makes it obvious why you see 120 results, and how to widen them.

Decision

Contact on the card

Call and WhatsApp buttons on every card turn browsing into a conversation without opening the listing.

03
Map

Search by neighbourhood, not by street name

For a buyer like Abdul, location comes first: near schools, hospitals and family. The map shows prices as pins, with swipeable cards along the bottom and a one-tap switch to a list.

04
Property detail

See it properly before you visit

The detail page answers the questions from our personas: what it really looks like, what's included, and what's nearby. Images, video and a 360 view sit in the first screen, and registering interest takes three fields.

Decision

Media before price

Photos, video and 360 view lead the page, because trust in the photos was the top worry in research.

Decision

Interest, not a call

Registering interest sends a lead to the seller's dashboard, so a buyer can enquire at midnight without a phone call.

Decision

Report a listing

A "Report property" link on every listing gives people a way to flag fakes, which helps the platform earn trust.

05
Pro workspace

A broker's whole week, in one dashboard

For Syed and his peers, the app replaces the phone log and the spreadsheet. The dashboard shows active listings, visits, click-through and leads, and every call, WhatsApp message or registered interest lands as a notification.

Decision

Count what brokers care about

Leads, calls and WhatsApp clicks sit next to views, so a broker can tell which listings actually make the phone ring.

Decision

Permissions per agent

Brokers and developers choose whether each agent can handle commercial, selling or renting, and can block access in one step.

Decision

Every lead has a source

Notifications name the property and the channel, so follow-up starts with context instead of "who is this?".

06
Add a property

Nine short steps, or one upload for a whole portfolio

Listing one property is a nine-step wizard with a visible counter and Save on every step. Developers with dozens of units can upload a document instead and track each file's review status.

07
Arabic version

Mirrored, not just translated

The layouts were built so they could flip from left-to-right to right-to-left without redesign. Navigation, tabs, carousels, charts and back arrows all mirror, while numbers and prices keep their usual reading order.

Design language

One deep plum, photos do the rest

A clean, light layout kept the code simple, the app fast on slow networks and the focus on the property photos, which Dar Al Arkan controls end to end.

What I learned

Design for both sides of the transaction. A buyer's "register interest" is only useful if it lands as a clear lead for the seller. Designing both ends together kept the two halves of the app consistent.

Plan for RTL from the first wireframe. Because the layouts were built to mirror from the start, the Arabic version was a set of careful checks rather than a redesign.

Competitor reviews earn their keep. Seven portals showed the same overloaded search form. That gave the team a shared reason to start simple.
